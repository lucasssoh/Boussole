# Changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`, and each release is a git tag (`v1.0.0`). Major releases
also get a code name, minor ones their date; patch releases have neither. Until `1.0.0`, a
minor release may break things and says so.

## Unreleased

### Added
- **The map of a track:** a track shows as a map rather than a grid. Its chapters are blocks linked by what comes before what, each with how many of its sessions are finished; a click on a chapter zooms into it, its sessions linked the same way; each block as high as its whole title needs. The blocks come in floor after floor, each link drawn just before the block it reaches; the one to go on with stands out and the map opens on it, finished blocks are checked, those not open yet locked. No link crosses another; on a phone the blocks get smaller and the map scrolls sideways when it must. *All the chapters* goes back. Each chapter has its colour, a band on its block, its bar and the links leaving it; its sessions carry it, on the map and on the first step of each session, so that you always know which chapter you are in. The page of a track puts what it is and where to go beside its map; the button says *Start* until something is begun.
- **Chapters follow one another:** a prerequisite in another chapter makes the whole chapter wait for that chapter, so that a chapter is finished before those that need it; in guided mode its sessions are locked until then, in free mode it is advised, and *Resume* goes to a chapter that waits for no other. The validator reports chapters that wait for one another (`F044`).
- **Profile:** one light page: your culture (your emblem large, your records, the route of the divisions), your specialties as pills, the achievements you reached as medals. **Specialties** have their emblem: a medallion like a lens in its mount, in a metal more noble tier after tier (iron, copper, bronze, brass, silver, gold, crystal), around an instrument of optics, from the reading stone to the prism; a ring around it fills up to the next tier, without divisions. A new division shows on the sky of its tier.
- **Emblems:** each tier of the culture has its own: a gem around an instrument of navigation, from the floating needle of the Curious to the armillary sphere of the Grand Sage, ornamented tier after tier (a trim, rivets, laurel, a graduated ring, an oil lamp, rays, a halo), in an aura that grows as you rise; the division stands under it in relief, V to I. The profile shows yours large, and below it the route of every division as its emblem, yours always in the middle and larger, those ahead greyed. The tiers page fits on one screen: the eight tiers on one line, yours twice as large, under each its points and, for Sage and Grand Sage, a mark per specialty they ask; the tier you choose, yours at first, says what it stands for. On a phone, a tier a row. The tiers start at round numbers (1 200, 4 900, 16 000, 50 000, 120 000, 230 000 and 400 000 points). On the profile and for a new division, the aura breathes and light crosses the gem, still when the system asks for less motion. The specialties have no emblems yet.
- **Sources:** `:::beyond`, *Beyond the course*, sets apart what does not come from the documents of a track and says where it comes from (a web source, general knowledge), so that it is never taken for what the teacher said. In a track with documents, a notion that cites no source (no `:pdf` page, no `:link`) is reported (`P014`). The playbooks ask for a source on every notion, wherever a definition, a result or a method is taken, and on every solution; tracks built from nothing above all.
- **Critical thinking**, a field of Boussole's own (`domain: critical-thinking`: epistemology, logic, zetetics, the scientific method, cognitive biases). Grand Sage asks for it at Specialist, besides the three specialties of Sage.
- **Achievements:** 19 achievements kept for good, each with levels (I, II, III…), shown on the profile: *Drop by drop* (days in a row), *Without a blot* (sessions without a mistake), *Twenty times on the loom* (missed at first, right when asked again), *The madeleine* (quick reviews without a mistake), *Ithaca* (tracks finished), *Trial by fire* (mock exams), *The well-rounded mind* (fields)… They earn no points; their icons are to come.
- **New division:** reaching a new division, or a new tier, is shown once, full screen: the former emblem gives way to the new one, with the next step ahead.
- **Score of a track:** its page shows how demanding it is and its field, and its points: earned, missed for good, still to play, out of all it gives. The end of a session counts first answers, questions asked again and those missed, with their points.
- **Profile:** your card (emblem, culture rank, level, points, best streak, flawless sessions, first try), the route of the divisions around yours, the next one, the fields Sage asks for, and your specialty in each field. **Tiers:** the culture, overall, division by division, beside one branch per field you study, its specialty from Novice to Luminary with where you stand and the points to the next division. The header leads there.
- **Course format:** `subject.yaml` says how demanding a track is (`level`: discovery, secondary, bachelor, master, research) and its field (`domain`, from the fields of the UNESCO classification ISCED-F 2013); the `cursus-plan` playbook sets them and shows them in the plan; without them, a warning (`P013`). `hook=true` marks a hook question, a guess in the warmup that earns no points (`F043` elsewhere).
- **Validator:** a `::ref` that asks a question of the same session or of a later one is an error (`F042`): a warmup or a review asks only about what was studied before.
- **`boussole index <folder>`:** every question of a track on one line, with how many times it is already asked again elsewhere: the agent picks its warmup and review questions there, the least asked first, so that warmups vary. The `AGENTS.md` installed in a track says how to write many sessions at once (one agent per chapter, each writing only in its folder) and which model suits which task.
- **Sessions that need others first:** a session whose `prerequisites` are not finished shows them when it opens, with a link to each; its card says them on hover, so that the cards stay light. In guided mode it is locked (a lock on its card, and it stays on its first step) until they are finished; in free mode they are only advised. *Resume* goes to a session whose prerequisites are finished.
- **`boussole open <folder> --resume`:** opens the session to resume of a track (the one in progress, or else the next one) rather than its page, for a reminder in your calendar that starts a session.
- **Authors, rights and credits:** a built-in track, in English and French, on respecting the work of others: who owns a course document, reading a licence (Creative Commons and others), citing and quoting, the credits Boussole shows (`written_by`, `authors`, `contributors`, sources and their licences), and sharing a track: whoever shares documents is responsible for having the right to. General information, not legal advice.
- **Who wrote it:** each session says who wrote, revised or translated it (`written_by`: the AI model by its exact ID and the tool, or a person, with the date), shown on its first step; a track credits its authors (people or organisations, such as a university) and contributors, shown on its page with the models it was written with. The playbooks fill it in; a session without it is reported (`P012`).
- **Translating a track:** the `track-translate` playbook writes a version of a track in another language next to it, translating only the text and pointing to the original's documents (`sources: "../python/sources"`, which Boussole now serves). *Writing a track by hand* teaches versions.
- **Course format, versions:** `boussole validate` compares a track with its versions in other languages (the folders next to it, the tracks of `config.yaml` with the same `id`): different IDs or a question that asks something else is an error (`F040`), a session not yet translated a warning (`F041`). `docs/FORMAT.md` §9 says how to write a version: only the text is translated.
- **Tracks in several languages:** the versions of a track in several languages (folders with the same `id`, each its own `lang`) show as one track. Pick the language on its page (« Follow in: English · Français »), or switch in a session (EN · FR in its bar): the same session opens at the same place. Progress is the same in every language. The built-in tracks come in all their languages, whatever the language of the interface.
- **Home:** under your tracks, the tracks built into Boussole sit on two shelves of their own colour: *How to Boussole* (the guide, writing a track by hand, rights and credits) and *Discover* (tracks on other subjects, to come).
- **Writing a track by hand:** a built-in track, in English and French, teaches the course format step by step for those who write a track without an agent: the folder, the plan, sessions, directives, questions, lexicon and sources, reviews, and how to read the validator; with one example built up across the sessions.
- **Tracks from nothing:** the `cursus-discover` playbook takes over a track started with `boussole new`: it asks what you want to be able to do, finds open sources (official documentation, university courses, open textbooks), downloads those whose licence allows it and sums up the others in source notes, records each with its address and licence, shows you the list, then makes the plan; every session cites its sources with links that open beside the text.
- **`boussole new <name> [--goal …]`:** starts a track without documents of your own: its folder (in `tracks_dir` of `config.yaml`, `~/Boussole` by default) is prepared for an agent and listed in Boussole, with your goal as its description.
- **Sourced tracks:** an entry of `cursus/sources.yaml` can say where its document comes from: `url`, `title`, `licence`, `retrieved`. A document from the web without its licence is reported (`F039`); a page that may not be copied is summed up in a source note, `web/<name>.md`, with its address.
- **Web references:** `::link[…]{url=…}`, or `:link[…]{url=…}` in a sentence, opens a web page the track cites beside the text: in place when the site allows it; a site that refuses opens at once in a window of its own, on the right, reused for every such reference (the links of a step are looked at as soon as it shows, so that a click knows where to open them). Boussole looks at a page only when you open it, never at your own machine or network.
- **Videos from your own files:** `::video[…]{src=…}` plays a video of the sources folder, such as a lecture recording, beside the text, from `start` to `end`, like a YouTube video. The validator checks the file exists, warns about formats browsers do not play (`F038`), and the document tracking follows videos too. The `video-find` playbook looks at the learner's recordings before YouTube.
- **Contributing:** `AGENTS.md` tells people and agents changing Boussole what each kind of change touches (code, format, diagrams, playbooks, guide, changelog), and `DocumentationSyncTest` fails the build when the docs drift from the code.
- **Course format:** options of a choice gap and right-hand sides of a match show in drop-down lists, which cannot draw maths: write them in plain text with Unicode symbols (≤, x₁), not `$…$`.
- **Course format, validation:** `boussole validate <folder>` checks a subject against the course format and lists each problem with its file, line and what to change. It ends with `0` when there is no error, so an agent writing a course can run it until it passes.
- **Agents:** `boussole init <folder>` turns a folder of course documents into a subject ready for an AI agent (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI…): it creates the subject's files and installs instructions that guide the agent from the plan of the course, which you approve, to each session, its exercises, its lexicon and its videos. Running it again updates the instructions and never touches your course or your documents.
- **Agents, your way of learning:** before anything else, the agent asks you a few multiple-choice questions (a quick quiz after each point? try before the example? close with a quiz, an exercise or both? how long a session?) and every session is then built that way. `boussole validate` warns when a session does not follow your answers. Kept in `~/.config/boussole/learner.yaml`, your answers are reused by `boussole init` for your other subjects.
- **Fill-in exercises:** a gap can now be a drop-down choice or a number, besides typed text. Proofs, algorithms and equations are asked as fill-ins where you pick each missing step, so you never have to type a formula.
- **Session player:** `boussole open <folder>` opens your subject in the browser. A session shows its goal and objectives, its notions, maths and diagrams; videos and your own PDFs (at the right page) open beside the text, and terms of the lexicon show their definition on hover. You answer MCQs by ticking, fill gaps by typing or picking, order and match items, and get the feedback of each wrong choice; hints come one at a time, and the solution once you have tried. Your progress is kept, and the course shows the sessions you have finished and where to continue.
- **Session player:** a session comes one screen at a time: its goal, then each explanation, worked example and question on its own screen, then a wrap-up. *Previous* and *Next* (or ← and →) move between them, and your answers stay when you come back. Beside it, the plan of the session shows where you are, what you have seen and how each question went.
- **Session player:** *Check* waits until every box of a fill-in or a matching is filled, and says so; an MCQ with several right answers asks you to confirm before your final answer.
- **Session player, putting in order:** each line has its own colour, kept wherever it moves, and its rank; drag it with the mouse or a finger, or use its arrows.
- **Session player:** in a lesson, your first answer to a question is final: coming back to it later (even on another day or another browser) shows that answer and its correction, and the path keeps its cross if it was wrong, so think before you answer. The server refuses a second answer. Reviews and mock exams can be sat again.
- **Session player:** in a lesson, *Next* waits until you have answered the question on screen and seen its correction (even wrong); practice exercises, free exercises, reviews and mock exams never hold you. `free_navigation: true` in `config.yaml` lifts it.
- **Look:** a rounded, colourful interface with buttons in relief, a colour per subject, and the session as a path of steps on the left, through a coloured zone per part (warm-up, each notion, checkpoint, practice) with its sign on the path, steps that turn green as you go, red or orange for a missed question, locked until you answer.
- **Look:** a logo, a compass needle in a rounded square, in the header, the browser tab and as the icon of the page saved on a phone.
- **Look:** entering a page or a screen, its elements pop in one after the other like bubbles, and the path of the session draws itself node after node (none of it when your system asks for less motion).
- **Experience, levels and streak:** each answer earns experience (more for a first answer, still some for a wrong one), finishing a session too; levels follow, and the header shows your streak of days in a row. The end of a session shows the experience earned.
- **Home:** the session to resume comes first (where you left off, or the next one), with the sessions of its chapter as a grid, then each subject with its progress and its next session. A subject's page shows all its sessions as a grid, chapter by chapter; each card shows how many of its questions you have answered, and how many right.
- **Session player:** you answer a question once; right after, the right answer turns green, yours red if it was wrong (with why), and a « Why » block beside the question explains the right answer. Before answering, you can reveal hints one at a time. In a mock exam, corrections come when the time is up or when you end it.
- **Course format, validation:** every MCQ needs hints too (P004), and every wrong choice of an MCQ its feedback (P010), since the learner sees them right after answering.
- **Session player, reviews and mock exams:** a timed session starts with its countdown and ends with the number of questions you got right at the first attempt.
- **Session player:** English or French, light or dark (in the small settings panel under the gear of the header), and usable on a phone.
- **Server:** `boussole open` starts it in the background when needed, `boussole stop` stops it (even when it no longer answers), `boussole serve` runs it in a terminal. It only answers this machine, and keeps working while a new version of Boussole is built.
- **Documents that arrive during the year:** `cursus/sources.yaml` records what the course did with each document of the sources folder (used, ignored, pending, with its SHA-256). `boussole validate` warns when a document is new, changed or gone, or sits in two places (sub-folders included), `boussole sources <folder>` lists them with the entries to add, and the agent's `sources-update` skill takes them into the course without renumbering or rewriting what is written.
- **Course format, graphs:** `:::graph` draws a small graph from lines of text (`a -> b : 3`, `a -- b`, `s "Source" @ 0,1`, `!` to make a node or edge stand out), placed on a circle, a row or in layers, or where the author says; Boussole checks it (`F037`, `P011` above 12 nodes) and draws it in the course colour, in light and dark themes. The skills tell agents when to use it.
- **Wording:** the interface names each feature after the problem it solves: a *track* (a school subject, but also a software manual, a company guide or a programming language), made of sessions of *steps*, followed on *the trail*; *nudges* rather than hints, *quick reviews*, *first try*, *final check*, *guided mode*.
- **Guide:** its lexicon holds the vocabulary of Boussole (track, step, Trail, guided mode, first try, nudge, final check, quick review, streak), defined on hover where the lessons use it; the validator keeps its name.
- **Guide:** each feature is introduced by the problem it solves (« Too many documents, no path? »), with the names of the interface: track, step, the Trail, nudges, first try, final check.
- **Guide:** its lessons are laid out in lists, steps, tables and callouts rather than long paragraphs, and tell the new rules: the first answer is final, several ticks are confirmed.
- **Guide:** « Getting started with Boussole », a short subject in English or French (the language of the interface) with a diagram of how documents become a course, the commands, the session player and documents that arrive later. A new Boussole opens on it; afterwards it stays apart on the home page, to read again when you forget, and earns no experience.
- **Course format, validation:** `boussole validate --check-videos` also asks YouTube whether each video exists.
- **Reviews and mock exams:** besides lessons, a course has timed sessions: a short review at the end of each chapter, and mock exams as long as the real ones. Their number of questions fits the time allowed, knowing that choosing an answer is quicker than building one; `boussole validate` warns when it does not.

### Changed
- **Smooth everywhere:** a page that waits for its content shows the needle of Boussole turning (only after a moment, so that a quick page never flickers), Boussole opens on its own splash, pages fade into one another, and leaving a chapter of the map steps back rather than cuts. The terms of the lexicon show their mathematics.
- **One theme, Brume:** a soft light ground for reading (a haze of blue, cards of an off-white, ink-blue text rather than black), the green of Boussole for what you do; the night is kept for the places of the game: the high tiers, the achievements, a new division. The light or dark setting is gone.
- **Opening a page:** the blocks pop in one after the other (cards, questions, framed blocks, tiers), the text around them no longer bounces.
- **Points and ranks** in place of experience: only what is right earns, a first answer missed stays missed. A value, a single-choice MCQ or an order earns 1; a fill 1 per sentence whose gaps are all right; a multiple-choice MCQ or a match shares its point between its elements, a wrong one taking its share back; a hook earns nothing. A question asked again in another session earns half, once. Points are multiplied by the level of the track (discovery ×1 to research ×4); a session finished without a mistake earns a tenth more. They climb two ladders: the culture (Curious, Apprentice, Initiate, Scholar, Savant, Master, Sage, each in divisions V to I, then Grand Sage without end; Sage asks for 3 specialties at Specialist, Grand Sage for critical thinking at Specialist besides), and a specialty in each field (Novice to Authority, then Luminary). Earlier answers keep what their first answer was worth.
- **Warmups:** besides questions on earlier sessions, a warmup may ask one hook question, a guess on what the session is about to teach: trying before the lesson, even wrongly, makes it stick better.
- **MCQs:** the choices are shown shuffled, the same way each time, like the options of a drop-down list: the place of the right choice no longer gives it away, whatever the order in the file.
- **Playbooks, after a review of the research on learning** (`docs/research/pedagogie.md`): warmups ask about the previous session **and** about older ones, before they are forgotten; reviews mix notions, types of question and earlier chapters, with a cumulative review halfway and before each exam; a question per notion changes the setting, the closing asks which method applies; *answer + reason* questions; help fades along a chapter (half-solved examples, fewer nudges in practice and reviews); the plan lists what each notion needs and the usual mistakes, which become the wrong choices of MCQs; a free exercise ends with points to check; the quick quiz is offered first, and the order (example or try first) follows where the learner starts from.
- **MCQs:** the `exercise-author` playbook now writes four choices, whose wrong ones are real mistakes that differ from the right one by a single detail, so that a question checks understanding rather than a quick read.

### Fixed
- **MCQs:** one choice per line, so that a formula has the whole width instead of being cut.
- **Drop-down lists** (choice gaps, matching) look like the rest of the interface: the arrow of Boussole, in relief, with a hover; « Choose… » reads as a prompt, not as an answer.
- **Course format, validation:** a YAML value broken by `: ` now says to put it in quotes. `docs/FORMAT.md` says it, and settles points left unclear: hints of a question written in an exam (allowed, shown when the question is asked again elsewhere), `:term` ignoring case, and the ID of a question written in a review (`r19-q1`).
- **Fill-in exercises:** a gap written in code (`` `ip [[1]] show` `` or a code block) is a field to fill, as the validator already counted it; elsewhere than a fill statement, `[[N]]` shows as written instead of vanishing.
- **Course format:** a task list inside fenced code, in the statement of an MCQ, is shown as code instead of being read as its choices.
