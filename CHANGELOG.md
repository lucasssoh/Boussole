# Changelog

Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`, and each release is a git tag (`v1.0.0`). Major releases
also get a code name, minor ones their date; patch releases have neither. Until `1.0.0`, a
minor release may break things and says so.

## Unreleased

### Added
- **To learn more:** Boussole can ship more than its guide: the home page lists the other built-in tracks under *To learn more*.
- **Writing a track by hand:** a built-in track, in English and French, teaches the course format step by step for those who write a track without an agent: the folder, the plan, sessions, directives, questions, lexicon and sources, reviews, and how to read the validator; with one example built up across the sessions.
- **Tracks from nothing:** the `cursus-discover` playbook takes over a track started with `boussole new`: it asks what you want to be able to do, finds open sources (official documentation, university courses, open textbooks), downloads those whose licence allows it and sums up the others in source notes, records each with its address and licence, shows you the list, then makes the plan; every session cites its sources with links that open beside the text.
- **`boussole new <name> [--goal …]`:** starts a track without documents of your own: its folder (in `tracks_dir` of `config.yaml`, `~/Boussole` by default) is prepared for an agent and listed in Boussole, with your goal as its description.
- **Sourced tracks:** an entry of `cursus/sources.yaml` can say where its document comes from: `url`, `title`, `licence`, `retrieved`. A document from the web without its licence is reported (`F039`); a page that may not be copied is summed up in a source note, `web/<name>.md`, with its address.
- **Web references:** `::link[…]{url=…}`, or `:link[…]{url=…}` in a sentence, opens a web page the track cites beside the text: in place when the site allows it; a site that refuses opens at once in a window of its own, on the right, reused for every such reference (the links of a step are looked at as soon as it shows, so that a click knows where to open them). Boussole looks at a page only when you open it, never at your own machine or network.
- **Videos from your own files:** `::video[…]{src=…}` plays a video of the sources folder, such as a lecture recording, beside the text, from `start` to `end`, like a YouTube video. The validator checks the file exists, warns about formats browsers do not play (`F038`), and the document tracking follows videos too. The `video-find` playbook looks at the learner's recordings before YouTube.
- **Contributing:** `AGENTS.md` tells people and agents changing Boussole what each kind of change touches (code, format, diagrams, playbooks, guide, changelog), and `DocumentationSyncTest` fails the build when the docs drift from the code.
- **Course format:** options of a choice gap and right-hand sides of a match show in drop-down lists, which cannot draw maths: write them in plain text with Unicode symbols (≤, x₁), not `$…$`.
- **Course format, validation:** `boussole validate <folder>` checks a subject against the course format and lists each problem with its file, line and what to change. It ends with `0` when there is no error, so an agent writing a course can run it until it passes.
- **Agents:** `boussole init <folder>` turns a folder of course documents into a subject ready for an AI agent (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI…): it creates the subject's files and installs instructions that guide the agent from the plan of the course, which you approve, to each session, its exercises, its lexicon and its videos. Running it again updates the instructions and never touches your course or your documents.
- **Agents, your way of learning:** before anything else, the agent asks you a few multiple-choice questions (a quick quiz after each point? try before the example? close with a quiz, an exercise or both? how long a session?) and every session is then built that way. `boussole validate` warns when a session does not follow your answers. Kept in `~/.config/boussole/learner.yaml`, your answers are reused by `boussole init` for your other subjects.
- **Fill-in exercises:** a gap can now be a drop-down choice or a number, besides typed text. Proofs, algorithms and equations are asked as fill-ins where you pick each missing step, so you never have to type a formula.
- **Session player:** `boussole open <folder>` opens your subject in the browser. A session shows its goal and objectives, its notions, maths and diagrams; videos and your own PDFs (at the right page) open beside the text, and terms of the lexicon show their definition on hover. You answer MCQs by ticking, fill gaps by typing or picking, order and match items, and get the feedback of each wrong choice; hints come one at a time, and the solution once you have tried. Your progress is kept, and the course shows the sessions you have finished and where to continue.
- **Session player:** a session comes one screen at a time: its goal, then each explanation, worked example and question on its own screen, then a wrap-up. *Previous* and *Next* (or ← and →) move between them, and your answers stay when you come back. Beside it, the plan of the session shows where you are, what you have seen and how each question went.
- **Session player:** *Check* waits until every box of a fill-in or a matching is filled, and says so; an MCQ with several right answers asks you to confirm before your final answer.
- **Session player, putting in order:** each line has its own colour, kept wherever it moves, and its rank; drag it with the mouse or a finger, or use its arrows.
- **Session player:** in a lesson, your first answer to a question is final: coming back to it later (even on another day or another browser) shows that answer and its correction, and the path keeps its cross if it was wrong, so think before you answer. The server refuses a second answer. Reviews and mock exams can be sat again.
- **Session player:** in a lesson, *Next* waits until you have answered the question on screen and seen its correction (even wrong); practice exercises, free exercises, reviews and mock exams never hold you. `free_navigation: true` in `config.yaml` lifts it.
- **Look:** a rounded, colourful interface with buttons in relief, a colour per subject, and the session as a path of steps on the left, through a coloured zone per part (warm-up, each notion, checkpoint, practice) with its sign on the path, steps that turn green as you go, red or orange for a missed question, locked until you answer.
- **Look:** a logo, a compass needle in a rounded square, in the header, the browser tab and as the icon of the page saved on a phone.
- **Look:** entering a page or a screen, its elements pop in one after the other like bubbles, and the path of the session draws itself node after node (none of it when your system asks for less motion).
- **Experience, levels and streak:** each answer earns experience (more for a first answer, still some for a wrong one), finishing a session too; levels follow, and the header shows your streak of days in a row. The end of a session shows the experience earned.
- **Home:** the session to resume comes first (where you left off, or the next one), with the sessions of its chapter as a grid, then each subject with its progress and its next session. A subject's page shows all its sessions as a grid, chapter by chapter; each card shows how many of its questions you have answered, and how many right.
- **Session player:** you answer a question once; right after, the right answer turns green, yours red if it was wrong (with why), and a « Why » block beside the question explains the right answer. Before answering, you can reveal hints one at a time. In a mock exam, corrections come when the time is up or when you end it.
- **Course format, validation:** every MCQ needs hints too (P004), and every wrong choice of an MCQ its feedback (P010), since the learner sees them right after answering.
- **Session player, reviews and mock exams:** a timed session starts with its countdown and ends with the number of questions you got right at the first attempt.
- **Session player:** English or French, light or dark (in the small settings panel under the gear of the header), and usable on a phone.
- **Server:** `boussole open` starts it in the background when needed, `boussole stop` stops it (even when it no longer answers), `boussole serve` runs it in a terminal. It only answers this machine, and keeps working while a new version of Boussole is built.
- **Documents that arrive during the year:** `cursus/sources.yaml` records what the course did with each document of the sources folder (used, ignored, pending, with its SHA-256). `boussole validate` warns when a document is new, changed or gone, or sits in two places (sub-folders included), `boussole sources <folder>` lists them with the entries to add, and the agent's `sources-update` skill takes them into the course without renumbering or rewriting what is written.
- **Course format, graphs:** `:::graph` draws a small graph from lines of text (`a -> b : 3`, `a -- b`, `s "Source" @ 0,1`, `!` to make a node or edge stand out), placed on a circle, a row or in layers, or where the author says; Boussole checks it (`F037`, `P011` above 12 nodes) and draws it in the course colour, in light and dark themes. The skills tell agents when to use it.
- **Wording:** the interface names each feature after the problem it solves: a *track* (a school subject, but also a software manual, a company guide or a programming language), made of sessions of *steps*, followed on *the trail*; *nudges* rather than hints, *quick reviews*, *first try*, *final check*, *guided mode*.
- **Home:** the guide sits beside the session to resume (under it on a phone).
- **Guide:** its lexicon holds the vocabulary of Boussole (track, step, Trail, guided mode, first try, nudge, final check, quick review, streak), defined on hover where the lessons use it; the validator keeps its name.
- **Guide:** each feature is introduced by the problem it solves (« Too many documents, no path? »), with the names of the interface: track, step, the Trail, nudges, first try, final check.
- **Guide:** its lessons are laid out in lists, steps, tables and callouts rather than long paragraphs, and tell the new rules: the first answer is final, several ticks are confirmed.
- **Guide:** « Getting started with Boussole », a short subject in English or French (the language of the interface) with a diagram of how documents become a course, the commands, the session player and documents that arrive later. A new Boussole opens on it; afterwards it stays apart on the home page, to read again when you forget, and earns no experience.
- **Course format, validation:** `boussole validate --check-videos` also asks YouTube whether each video exists.
- **Reviews and mock exams:** besides lessons, a course has timed sessions: a short review at the end of each chapter, and mock exams as long as the real ones. Their number of questions fits the time allowed, knowing that choosing an answer is quicker than building one; `boussole validate` warns when it does not.
